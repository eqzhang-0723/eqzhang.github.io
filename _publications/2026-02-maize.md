  <summary><i class="fas fa-angle-down"></i> Abstract</summary>

Understanding how infrastructure inequality shapes food access is vital for guiding public transport investment, especially where food insecurity is already severe and when international food aid shrinks. We contribute by examining how road infrastructure can mitigate the global shock of oil prices on local maize markets in sub-Saharan Africa. The analysis combines real time maize prices in 20 African countries, international oil price movements associated with the Russia Ukraine war, and road networks that connect markets to ports. We find that when global oil prices increased from \$74 to \$123 per barrel (as it did between December 2021 and June 2022), local maize prices increased by 40.03\% relative to otherwise comparable markets located 500 km closer to ports. Meanwhile, small-scale conflicts in these more distant markets rose by 40.72\%. Our results also show that in agriculturally suitable areas, transport costs have a weaker effect on maize price increases but a stronger effect on the escalation of conflicts. These findings underscore the importance of extending road networks into interior regions to improve food access and mitigating food shortage.
